# Explainable Trace Clustering via Behaviour–Context Decoupling

Reproducibility package for the evaluation of a **decoupled** internal process-benchmarking
methodology on the public **BPI Challenge 2019** purchase-to-pay event log.

The method keeps process behaviour and business context in two separate spaces:

1. **Split** the log into a *behavioural* space (how a case ran) and a *static context* space
   (what the case is — procurement metadata, held out of clustering).
2. **Cluster** on behaviour only → behavioural cohorts with complete coverage.
3. **Rank** the cohorts per business KPI to surface the reference and laggard cohorts.
4. **Attribute** cohort membership *post hoc* with a context-only classifier.
5. **Explain** each cohort with SHAP feature–value drivers over the excluded context space.

Because the cohorts are formed from behaviour alone, the context factors that characterise them
*emerge* from the data instead of being pre-selected. All attributions are **associative, not
causal**.

## Running

Everything runs from one call:

```python
import sys, os
sys.path.insert(0, "src")

from orchestrator import run_pipeline

results = run_pipeline(k=6, force_reparse=False, weighting="balanced")
```

`run_pipeline` resolves paths relative to the repository root by default; pass `repo_root=` to
override. The first run stream-parses the raw XES and caches it to parquet (slow); every rerun
reuses the cache.

Notebook `03_pipeline.ipynb` regenerates every artifact, and `04_insights.ipynb` produces the SHAP
drivers and figures. Both run from a clean clone once the XES is in place.

Notebooks `01` and `02` are the **exploratory record** of how the two feature sets were chosen.
They read cached intermediate tables (`data/interim/bpi2019_process_only.csv`,
`bpi2019_static.csv`) that were produced during development and are not regenerated by `src/`, so
they are included for transparency rather than as a runnable path. Their conclusions are persisted
in `artifacts/clustering_feature_search.csv` and `artifacts/classification_eda.json`.

## Feature spaces

**Behavioural (11 engineered features, clustered):** `total_events`, `unique_activities`,
`n_resources`, `n_goods_receipts`, `has_payment_block_removed`, `has_change_activity`,
`has_cancel_activity`, `has_delete_po_item`, `automation_ratio`, `repetition_ratio`,
`handoff_ratio`.

Bigram TF-IDF over activity transitions (capped at 30) is computed as well, but is **excluded from
the clustering distance** — it degrades the silhouette and is used only to narrate cohorts after
the fact.

**Static context (10 features, held out of clustering, used only for attribution):**
`item_category`, `item_type`, `spend_area`, `sub_spend_area`, `spend_classification`,
`document_type`, `company`, `gr_based_inv_verif`, `goods_receipt`, plus numeric
`case_value_eur_log`. Vendor and material identifiers are excluded as high-cardinality
memorisation risks.

Exact formulas are in `src/data_preparation.py` (`build_behavioural_features`,
`build_context_features`).

## KPIs

| KPI | Definition | Aggregation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cycle time | Case duration in days | median |
| Automation | Share of events performed by non-human accounts | mean |
| Rework | Case contains a cancel, change or delete activity | mean (rate) |
| Payment block | Case had a payment block removed | mean (rate) |
| Events | Number of events in the case | mean |

Durations use the median because BPIC 2019 contains implausible extreme timestamps; rates use the
mean. Definitions live in `compute_case_kpis` / `rank_cohorts` in `src/data_modelling.py`.

## Reference results

Log: 251,734 cases · 1,595,923 events · 42 activities · 11,973 control-flow variants.

**Clustering** — k-means on z-scored behavioural features, `k=6`:
silhouette **0.478**, Calinski–Harabasz 74,238, Davies–Bouldin 1.06.

| Cohort | Share | Cycle (d, median) | Autom. | Rework | Block | Events | Ctx. F1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C0 Standard P2P | 58.7% | 66.1 | 0.26 | 0.11 | 0.00 | 5.4 | 0.77 |
| C1 PO-deletion | 3.5% | 3.1 | 0.08 | 1.00 | 0.00 | 2.7 | 0.21 |
| C2 Rework/cancellations | 5.8% | 85.7 | 0.42 | 0.66 | 0.32 | 14.3 | 0.42 |
| C3 Short / GR-only | 11.4% | 15.9 | 0.07 | 0.16 | 0.00 | 2.4 | 0.72 |
| C4 High-volume service-entry | 0.3% | 0.1 | 0.92 | 0.15 | 0.04 | 200.6 | 0.53 |
| C5 Payment-block removed | 20.3% | 83.7 | 0.25 | 0.14 | 1.00 | 6.5 | 0.31 |

**Contextual attribution** — XGBoost with native categorical handling, 70/30 stratified split,
balanced class weights, 500 trees / depth 6 / lr 0.1 / subsample 0.85:
accuracy **0.655**, macro-F1 **0.492**, weighted-F1 **0.629**, against a class-prior baseline
macro-F1 of 0.169.

A high overall F1 is *not* the objective — a near-perfect one would mean behaviour is redundant.
What matters is the **spread** across cohorts (`Ctx. F1` above): structural cohorts (C0, C3) are
well explained by static context, while rework/exception cohorts (C1, C2, C5) are not. That gap is
precisely the evidence that behaviour carries information context cannot, and therefore the
evidence for decoupling.

## Reproducibility

Seeds are fixed for k-means, the train/test split and the classifier, so a rerun on the same input
reproduces the numbers above. Silhouette is estimated on a sample for tractability.
